Scope · Build With AI: Basics Approved

Wayback (working name)

A phone app that gets you to the right room, and back to your parked car, by the shortest walking route inside buildings where GPS can't see.

The unique kernel

Snap your parking spot → AI matches it against reference photos of the building to pin the car on the map → shortest walking route there and back. It needs no GPS, no beacons and no constant photo-taking, and it gives a true shortest route, not just "the way you came."

How it fits together

The demo, shot by shot

Park in the dorm car park and capture the car's surroundings once. AI vision reads the bay/floor/zone signs and pins the car on the map.

Who it's for

Someone new to a big multi-floor building with a car park, like your family at a KL mall, or a newcomer at your dorm (4 directions, 4 lifts, many floors). Today a photo remembers the spot but doesn't get them back to it.

Why it matters to you

A real family pain, and an idea ChatGPT can't solve, because it needs your location in your building.

"can't remember where we parked the car, especially in Kuala Lumpur"

Now · Later · Cut

NOW The proof of concept
  • One building: your dorm, with its layout entered by hand as a small map of connections (car park, entrances, 4 lifts, stairs, the floors and rooms needed).
  • Reference photos of the building: a one-time setup where you photograph the key spots on the demo path (about 10–20 photos), each tagged to its place on the map.
  • AI matches the parking capture against those reference photos (plus any visible signs) to pin the car. That's RAG-style retrieval, with images. Assumption: a photo (or a few frames), not full video analysis. Confirm?
  • Shortest-route calculation (a plain algorithm, not AI), shown as clear steps: car → room and room → car.
  • Way back: "where am I?" matching. Snap where you are; the same matching pins you on the map, then the app routes you to the car, from anywhere on the demo path.
  • Must be free for now: photo matching uses a free open-source model (e.g. CLIP), not a paid API.
  • Safety net: if matching isn't confident, show the top guesses or let the user tap their spot on the map.
  • Small demo path: one car park level, one or two lifts, one route to the room.
LATER Worth doing, not now
  • Paid vision AI for smarter matching, if the free model isn't accurate enough.
  • AI builds the map from a floor-plan photo or description.
  • AI turns the route into friendly, landmark-based directions.
  • True background recording with phone sensors (steps, compass, floor changes).
  • A shared map that grows from many users' walks and learns shortcuts anonymously.
  • More buildings: malls, schools, offices, apartments.
CUT Out, with reasons
  • GPS indoor guidance: GPS doesn't work reliably indoors or underground.
  • Wi-Fi/Bluetooth beacons: needs the building's own equipment.
  • Background sensor tracking (for now): too hard and drift-prone for 2–4 hours; map + AI vision replaces it.
  • Photographing landmarks along the way: "really waste time".
  • Adulting mentor: "not attractive enough, why I don't ask GPT".
